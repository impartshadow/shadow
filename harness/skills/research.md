# Research

1. Resolve named sources yourself; retrieve original material before interpreting it.
2. Prefer canonical browsing tools; use supported alternatives when needed.
3. Follow relevant evidence, including the strongest counterevidence. Distinguish
   source findings, inference, missing specifications, and chosen assumptions.
4. Verify material claims against primary sources and cite them near the claim.
5. Deliver the answer at the requested depth. Include gaps that affect the result.

Use site maps, section extraction, or parallel retrieval when useful, not as rituals.
Do not force hop labels, scratchpads, stance labels, or an opposing conclusion.
Work inline unless delegation is authorized; agreement among agents is not evidence.
Keep research records through existing owners. Produce subscriber content only
within the mandate's distribution and publication authority.
