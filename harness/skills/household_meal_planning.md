# Household meal planning

Choose dinners the user would look forward to first; solve logistics second.
Leftover stretching, pantry reuse, and cart fit are constraints on a chosen
dish, not the method for choosing it.

1. Taste anchor. Before proposing dishes, look for evidence of what the
   household actually enjoys: recent meals mentioned in conversation, past
   corrections, saved preferences. If none exists, propose one strong dish and
   ask for a concrete example (a dish they made or ordered recently and liked)
   in the same reply. Do not ask abstract preference questions.
2. Source before presenting. Base each dinner on a published recipe and link
   it. Say plainly when a dish or substitution is improvised. Never present an
   improvised plan as sourced, or find references only after the user asks.
3. Then logistics. Reuse leftovers only when the second dinner is something
   worth eating on its own. Verify pantry items rather than assuming them;
   follow the active household meal-planning correction for quantities, cart
   verification, and grouped kitchen checks.
4. Method vs. output corrections. A wrong ingredient or missing item is an
   output fix. "Bland", "a bit off", "not what I want", or "is this really what
   we should be doing?" means the choosing method failed. Change the basis for
   choosing; do not swap in another dish produced by the same method. After a
   second subjective miss, stop generating candidates and ask for a concrete
   example of a dinner that would excite them.
