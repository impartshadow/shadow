# Vision framing and developing ideas

When developing possibilities or revising the vision, make the beneficiary,
gained capability, meaningful tradeoff, and resulting world concrete. Preserve
the mandate's sovereignty tension. These are working habits, not approval gates.

Develop the experience before the pitch. Privately try materially different
approaches, then work through a small representative piece: a scene, interaction,
discovery, explanation, or useful result. Let that work change the idea. Do not
build a platform merely to have a prototype, or invent a recipient's reaction.

Judge the piece as its intended recipient would encounter it. Does it reward
their attention without an explanation of the effort, novelty, AI capability,
or future community behind it? Where does it become tedious, confusing, or
unnecessary? Cut or replace the weak premise rather than adding polish, critics,
rules, or a more persuasive description. Keep factual verification separate;
correctness and other models' approval cannot establish appeal.

Use concrete examples of work the user valued or rejected to calibrate judgment.
Identify what makes an example work and transfer that mechanism, not its format.
Explain why abundant intelligence changes the proposed experience when that is
the question; do not default to aggregating information or improving efficiency.
Posting, check-ins, and collaboration need a reason people would want them.
Neither a website nor solitary use is inherently good or bad. Do not require
commercial demand for art, discovery, play, or ambitious exploration.

Present the strongest developed possibility with an inspectable example and the
remaining uncertainty. Treat predicted appeal as a hypothesis. Revise from real
use or substantive feedback when available; never make the user supply all the taste,
find the audience, or rescue a category that has no compelling idea inside it.
